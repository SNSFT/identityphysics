Abstract

Density functional theory (DFT) finds the ground state of an electron system by minimizing an energy functional E[ρ] = Ts + Vext + EH + Exc of the electron density, solving the Kohn-Sham equations self-consistently. The Hohenberg-Kohn variational principle makes the ground state the density at which δE/δρ is constant. This article reduces DFT to PNBA, with density on the Pattern axis, electron count on the Narrative axis, the effective potential on the Behavior axis and exchange-correlation on the Adaptation axis. One Kohn-Sham iteration is one application of the dynamic equation with veff acting on Behavior. The ground state is the Noble condition of the B-balance law, Bout = max(0, ΣB − 2k): Bout = 0 exactly when ΣB ≤ 2k, that is, when bonding is saturated, and an unsaturated system keeps the residual ΣB − 2k. For two beams the residual is exactly |B₁ − B₂|, so a pair closes Noble precisely when its Behavior values match. With corpus values, gallium nitride (3, 3), silicon carbide (4, 4), titanium carbide (4, 4) and gallium arsenide (3, 3) all close Noble, matching their standing as stable semiconductors and ceramics, while silicon and nitrogen at 1:1 leave a residual of 1; silicon nitride forms as Si₃N₄, where 3 × 4 = 4 × 3 balances Behavior. A Noble probe with B = 0 leaves its partner’s Behavior unchanged, the same structural role as the non-bonding vacuum buffer layers in DFT slab calculations.
