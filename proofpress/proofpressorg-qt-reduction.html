Abstract

Quantum teleportation (Bennett et al. 1993) moves an unknown quantum state from Alice to Bob using a shared entangled pair, a joint Bell measurement by Alice, two classical bits sent to Bob, and a correction gate applied by Bob. This article reduces each step to PNBA. The Bell pair is an additive Narrative fusion, NA + NB equal to a shared purpose vector, so measuring Alice’s half fixes Bob’s without any signal passing between them. The Bell measurement is a Behavior-triggered event whose torsion zone selects the correction. The classical channel is not a protocol choice: off the anchor the Identity Mass Suppression gate zeroes the output, so the correction must travel on a substrate. Bob’s correction is an Adaptation operation. No-cloning follows from Noble invariance: Noble fused with Noble stays Noble, and no Behavior can be extracted from a B = 0 state. The fidelity of the channel is F = 1 − τ with τ = Bnoise/Pchannel: a Noble channel teleports perfectly, and any noise strictly lowers fidelity. Against the best classical measure-and-prepare fidelity of 2/3 (Massar and Popescu 1995), a channel beats the classical limit exactly when τ < 1/3. Every Locked channel, τ below TL ≈ 0.1369, therefore beats it with fidelity above 0.863. The Micius ground-to-satellite experiment (Ren et al. 2017) measured F = 0.80 ± 0.01; across that whole band τ lies between 0.19 and 0.21, past the torsion limit and so a Shatter channel, yet below 1/3, so it beats the classical limit, as observed.

  1. The torsion limit is TL = 0.136899099984016; the anchor has zero impedance; off the anchor the gate is red and the purpose vector is zeroed (torsion_limit_value, anchor_zero_friction, ims_lockdown, ims_anchor_gives_green, ims_drift_gives_red).
