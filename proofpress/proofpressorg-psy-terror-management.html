Abstract

Terror Management Theory (Greenberg, Solomon and Pyszczynski) holds that awareness of death creates potential terror, which people manage through a cultural worldview and self-esteem. Reminders of death (mortality salience) trigger two kinds of defense. Proximal defenses push conscious death thoughts away quickly. Distal defenses bolster worldview and self-esteem over time. High self-esteem buffers the effect. This article reduces the theory to PNBA. Worldview coherence and self-esteem are Pattern, symbolic immortality and cultural continuity are Narrative, the threat response is Behavior, and the capacity to integrate the threat is Adaptation. Mortality salience is Fext on Behavior, so it always raises torsion. Proximal defense lowers Behavior directly. Distal defense raises Pattern and Adaptation, lowering torsion and raising Identity Mass. Equanimity is a true lock. Terror is Shatter. Worldview rigidity, defended but without meaning, is a false lock: low torsion from Pattern overdrive with Narrative depleted below threshold.
