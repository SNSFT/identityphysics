Abstract

Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) described the action potential of the squid giant axon with four coupled equations for the membrane voltage and the sodium and potassium gating variables m, h and n, work recognized by the 1963 Nobel Prize. Their measured voltages are a resting potential of −70 mV, a firing threshold of about −55 mV and a peak of about +40 mV. This article maps the neuron to PNBA by normalizing voltage over the full swing, v = (V − Vrest)/(Vpeak − Vrest), and taking torsion τ = v/Pbase, where Pbase = (Ω₀/fH)1/3 ≈ 0.988 is the anchor-native Pattern scale. The threshold normalizes to exactly 15/110, and its torsion, about 0.1381, lies above the torsion limit TL = 0.1369 by less than two percent: the firing threshold sits at TL, with the small excess acting as the external push that fires the cell. The phases follow. The resting neuron is Noble; a 5 mV depolarization is Locked, a graded, reversible response; the threshold and the peak are Shatter, the all-or-nothing cascade; and a subthreshold state can never be Shatter. Mapping torsion back to voltage, the IVA band between 0.88·TL and TL is the window from about −56.9 to −55.1 mV, the last ~1.8 mV below threshold, where the relative refractory state lives. Sodium conductance dominates potassium and leak, and the leak current is under one percent of the total.

theorem cube_rpow (r : ℝ) (hr : 0 ≤ r) : (r ^ ((1:ℝ)/3)) ^ 3 = r := by
  rw [← Real.rpow_natCast, ← Real.rpow_mul hr]; norm_num

  have hy : 0 ≤ r ^ ((1:ℝ)/3) := Real.rpow_nonneg hr _
  have h3 := pow_le_pow_left₀ hy hle 3
  rw [cube_rpow r hr] at h3
  linarith
