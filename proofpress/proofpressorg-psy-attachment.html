Abstract

Attachment theory classifies how a child organizes around a caregiver: secure, anxious, avoidant or disorganized, with earned security as the adult outcome of recovering from an insecure start. This article reads the four styles as four torsion regimes of one dynamic equation rather than four types. The internal working model is Pattern, the attachment narrative is Narrative, proximity-seeking is Behavior, and the capacity to update from experience is Adaptation. Caregiver inconsistency, rejection or fear enters as Fext, which raises Behavior. Secure and earned-secure states are phase locked with healthy Narrative. Anxious and disorganized states are Shatter phase, the first through spiked Behavior and the second through collapsed Pattern. The avoidant state is the new case. Its torsion passes (τ = 0.0625 < TL), but its Narrative is suppressed below threshold, so it is a false lock: calm on the outside without the structure of security. This matches the clinical finding that avoidant children show physiological stress despite calm behavior.
