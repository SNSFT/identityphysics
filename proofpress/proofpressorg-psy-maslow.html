Abstract

Maslow’s hierarchy orders human needs from physiological survival through safety, belonging and esteem to self-actualization, with self-transcendence added in his late work. Its core claim is that lower needs must be substantially met before higher needs become motivating. This article reduces the six levels to PNBA regimes. The structural foundation from physiological and safety needs is Pattern. The belonging story, “do I matter?”, is Narrative. Competence and achievement are Behavior. Growth is Adaptation. Unmet need enters as Fext. Deficit levels are Shatter phase: physiological deprivation at τ = 1.67, safety deprivation at τ = 0.51. Growth levels are phase locked: belonging at 0.123, esteem at 0.118. Self-actualization is a true lock with full PNBA. Transcendence is a true lock with Adaptation above 1, the IVA gain. The move from Shatter to lock is the move from deficit to growth, and the ordering of levels follows from the torsion law: under a deficit load, Pattern below its floor forces Shatter.
