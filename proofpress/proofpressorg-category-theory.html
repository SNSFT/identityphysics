Abstract

Category theory studies structure through objects and the morphisms between them, subject to identity and associativity laws, and builds functors, natural transformations, adjunctions and monads on top. This article shows that PNBA identity states form a category and places the standard categorical constructions on the torsion scale. The objects are states (P, N, B, A) with P > 0 and B ≥ 0; a morphism is a torsion transition that shifts Behavior by δ. The identity is the zero shift, composition adds shifts, and the identity and associativity laws hold as equalities of morphisms. The shift is fixed by the endpoints, δ = Btarget − Bsource, so the category is thin: between two states there is at most one morphism. The Noble state at the anchor-native scale Pbase = (Ω₀/fH)1/3 ≈ 0.988 is terminal, since every state has exactly one morphism into it. The torsion map τ = B/P characterizes Noble exactly (τ = 0 if and only if B = 0) and, at fixed P, recovers Behavior. Placing the standard structures on this scale gives an ordering: the category of sets and the Yoneda embedding are Noble; groups, vector spaces, adjunctions and monads sit in Locked; ∞-categories are Shatter, past the torsion limit.
