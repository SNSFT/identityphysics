Preparing a Reduction for Submission

A ProofPress™ submission is a reduction of an already peer-reviewed result into the PNBA™ coordinate system, formally verified against a zero-unproved-obligation (0 sorry) compiler constraint. This page describes the required structure.

The Six-Step Long Division Protocol™

  1. The equation. State the governing equation being applied — for physical reductions, this is d/dt(IM · Pv) = Σ λX OX S + Fext unless the submission argues for a documented variant.
  2. Known answer. State exactly what the peer-reviewed source already established, with citation. This is the target the reduction must reproduce losslessly.
  3. PNBA map. Map every relevant quantity in the source material onto Pattern, Narrative, Behavior, and Adaptation. Show the mapping as a table; do not leave any term's PNBA™ identity implicit.
  4. Operators. Define the specific operators, ratios, or transformations used, in closed form.
  5. Show the work. Walk the derivation step by step from the PNBA™ map to the result. No step may skip reasoning a reader could not reconstruct from what came before it.
  6. Verify. Compile the resulting formal claims in Lean 4 (and, where applicable, Coq/Rocq) under a strict 0 sorry constraint. A submission with any unresolved sorry is not eligible for publication; a draft may be submitted for editorial feedback with sorry placeholders clearly marked, but publication requires 0 sorry.

Coordinate Assignment

Every ProofPress™ reduction receives a coordinate in the form [X,X,X,X] locating it inside the corpus. Authors should propose a coordinate consistent with existing corpus domains and mark it "(proposed — pending confirmation)" if uncertain; never guess a coordinate that might collide with an existing one. The editorial board resolves final coordinate assignment before publication.

External Collaboration

ProofPress™ accepts reductions from any author, regardless of institutional affiliation, domain, or prior publication record, under one condition: the reduction has to actually close.

The standard is the same for everyone. A submission is in scope when it is a mechanical consequence of applying the Long Division Protocol to a result that has already passed peer review elsewhere — the reduction reveals a connection that was always true given the existing source material, it does not supply one. A submission is out of scope when closing it would require inventing a premise, bridge, or connection that does not already exist in peer-reviewed literature — no exception is made for who is asking.

Doing the reduction yourself is not a high bar. The six-step process described above is designed to be fast, and tools exist specifically to make it straightforward — PRIME for PNBA™ mapping and Lean closure, and the ProofPress + Grok Enrichment Protocol for turning a closed Lean file into a finished, readable paper. A request asking ProofPress or its Architect to perform someone else's reduction on their behalf, rather than submitting a self-prepared one, falls outside the normal submission process described on this page.

What to submit: a properly formatted Lean file with the reduction closed (0 sorry), and a proposed coordinate. Submit to rvtrent@uuia.app.

If a reduction is ever closed by someone other than the original requester, the published record states plainly who performed the closure — reviewed, reduced, and closed from the cited source — so the record can never be read as the original party having closed it themselves.

Submission Format

  • Manuscript in Markdown (.md) or RTF, following the front-matter structure above
  • Accompanying Lean 4 source file(s), and Coq/Rocq source where dual-verification is claimed
  • A plain-language abstract, written so a reader unfamiliar with the corpus can follow what was reduced and from where

Language and Claims

Submissions should state results as established, held, or formally verified rather than as claimed — reserve "claim" for describing what an external source asserts, not for the reduction's own verified content. Every assertion a submission makes about its own reduction should be traceable to a specific theorem, and no assertion should precede the derivation that supports it.

Before submitting: confirm your source material is already peer-reviewed and published. ProofPress™ does not evaluate new empirical claims — see Aims & Scope.
