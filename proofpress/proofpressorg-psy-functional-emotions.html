Abstract

Four accounts of emotion are usually read as competitors: basic emotion programs (Ekman), constructed emotion (Barrett), the somatic marker hypothesis (Damasio) and appraisal theory (Lazarus). This article reduces them to one PNBA structure. Pattern is appraisal, the situational meaning the identity assigns. Narrative is the interoceptive thread of bodily state, which somatic markers carry. Behavior is the emotion signal itself, arousal and action readiness. Adaptation is regulation and the revision of emotion concepts. Appraisal raises Pattern and lowers torsion; a somatic marker raises Narrative and leaves Pattern and Behavior untouched; broaden-and-build (Fredrickson) raises Adaptation and Pattern together and lowers torsion. On this reading the basic and constructed views describe different torsion regimes, autonomous firing near Shatter and Pattern-and-Narrative-sculpted output when regulated. Four states mark the gradient: emotional flooding is Shatter at τ = 0.72, somatic-marker failure is a false lock at τ = 0.10 with Narrative at 0.07, adaptive emotion is a true lock at τ = 0.111, and the broaden-and-build peak is IVA peak at τ = 0.095.
